FORCING GOD INTO ARMAGEDDON

NATİQ MİSİRZADƏ · English

On a night when war, belief and geopolitical interests intertwine, questions grow in the narrator’s mind: who benefits from chaos, why do people merely watch approaching catastrophe, and what happens when humanity tries to interfere with fate?

FORCING GOD INTO ARMAGEDDON

The night is very quiet.

But this silence is not peace… It is that strange, artificial calm that comes before a storm.

The television has been left on. I have turned the volume down, but the images keep flowing. Explosions… people running… children crying… and experts who never stop talking. Each of them says something different, yet a single sentence keeps circling inside me:

“Which side are you on?..”

There are two voices inside my head.

One says:
“You’re exaggerating. The world has always been like this. Wars begin and end. The balance of power is built on precisely this.”

The other will not be quiet:
“No… this time is different.”

Even children know that Israel forced America into this war.

Because this time, it feels as though there is more than just a war.
It is as if the chessboard has grown… the pieces have multiplied… and no one is making only their own move anymore.

Some are eliminating people who might have been able to sit at the negotiating table. Some are targeting energy routes. Some are fanning the wind instead of putting out the fire.

And the most frightening part…
Millions of people are watching it all.

Some talk in cafés:
“You’ll see, this is going to escalate.”

Others explain it in academic language:
“These are clashes of geopolitical interests. Don’t exaggerate.”

But I…
I feel caught somewhere between the two.

Because this is what I see:
Everyone could be right.

And that possibility… is even more unsettling.

There is a thought that troubles me.
A thought that is never spoken aloud, yet quietly circulates beneath everything:

“If chaos grows, order becomes easier to establish.”

This idea… is one of the most dangerous ideas in history.

What Israel is trying to do seems clear: its leaders are intent on rendering all the surrounding countries ineffective. The more countries join the war, the more weak little states will remain around it. The people of Israel, meanwhile, await the coming of the Messiah as part of their faith. According to this view, the greater the events become, the more chaos they create, the more easily divine order will be established.

In short, they have an idea known as “forcing God into the apocalypse.”

Then another thought comes to mind…
Beliefs.

The things people believe in…
They can give people hope, but they can also bring catastrophe.

If some people truly believe
“That something much greater must happen”…

Then this is no longer merely a matter of politics.
It is the human desire to play with fate.

And what, then, is the religious meaning of this belief?

And when a person…

begins forcing an ending they did not write,
the greatest destruction follows.

I walk to the window.

The city is still the same.
Cars pass. People laugh. Somewhere, someone is drinking tea.

But there is a strange feeling inside me:

As though everyone, by behaving normally,
is denying that something abnormal is approaching.

I ask myself:

“Are we really safe?”

There is no answer.

Because this is no longer just one country.
Not one city.
Not one border.

The issue… is a chain.

And wherever its first link was broken,
it feels as though the final link will snap there too.

They say Gaza…
They say Lebanon…
They say Iran…

But in truth, everyone is whispering the same thing:

“This fire will spread.”

And here is the most frightening part:

No one really seems
to be trying to put it out.

Everyone is either watching…
or commenting…
or waiting for their turn. Sadly, that is the worst part of all.

I rest my head against the wall.

A prayer passes through my mind… Everyone has a prayer for the holiday, but I cannot put mine into words.

Because this time, the wish is not simple.

This time, the wish is not merely
“let us be saved.”

This time, the wish is this:

My prayer is to enter the next holiday without Israel.

Because wars do not begin with weapons.

First, they begin in minds.
Then they turn into sentences.
Then into beliefs…
and finally… they become reality.

I close my eyes.

The voice inside me says one last thing:

“If everyone believes they are right…
no one will stop.”

And then…

No one truly wins.

There is a saying: “If I speak, they will kill me; if I stay silent, I will die.” If death is inevitable in the end, staying silent would have been the more logical choice…
