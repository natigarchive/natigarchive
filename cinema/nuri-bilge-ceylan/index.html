Anahtar Kelimeler: Sinema, Türk Sineması, Nuri Bilge Ceylan, yazarın sineması, bağımsız sinema, yavaş sinema, film analizi ABSTRACT This study aims to reveal the transformation of the cinematic language in the films of Nuri Bilge Ceylan, who holds a significant place in Turkish cinema. In the first part of the research, the socio-political events that took place in Turkey during the 1990s and their impact on cinema are examined. In this context, the emergence of the independent cinema movement in Turkey is discussed, and the concept of narrative in cinema is analyzed within the framework of modern narrative cinema in order to obtain more comprehensive analytical results. The second part of the study focuses on Ceylan’s films produced during his independent filmmaking period—Koza, Kasaba, and Mayıs Sıkıntısı—and examines their contribution to the development of Turkish cinema as well as their visibility and recognition in national and international film festivals. In the third and final part, the director’s filmography is analyzed with a focus on the early stages of his cinematic development. One of the main assumptions of the study is that the elements shaping Ceylan’s film language emphasize static and aesthetically composed photographic images rather than traditional story-driven narration. Having started his career as a photographer, Ceylan brought a photographic perspective into his filmmaking, particularly in the narrative structure of his early works. As a result, the study suggests that Ceylan’s cinema constructs its narrative by combining the codes of both modern and classical cinema while reflecting the characteristics of an auteur director. His cinematic language evolves from silent and static images toward more dialogic and expressive visual storytelling, initially grounded in photographic realism. Over time, this structure gradually shifts, where minimal narration becomes less dominant and a more attentive and reflective cinematic language emerges. Furthermore, while addressing the existing social structure, Ceylan’s film language increasingly develops into a critical perspective on broader economic and political dynamics.
